5. Platform Architecture module cards

The six module cards (Custom LLM Training, Vector Infrastructure, Multi-Agent Systems, Predictive Intelligence, Air-Gapped Deployment, API-First Architecture) are well organized but stay abstract — each one describes a capability, not an outcome. Add one concrete "in practice" line to each card, under the existing description, before the "Explore Module →" link.

Custom LLM Training
Fine-tuning and full-parameter training of open-source models tailored to your industry's taxonomy and workflows.
→ In practice: your model learns your internal terminology, product catalog, and edge cases instead of guessing at them.

Vector Infrastructure
Enterprise-grade RAG pipelines with semantic caching, hybrid search, and sub-millisecond retrieval latency.
→ In practice: your team (or your agents) can ask a natural-language question and get an answer sourced from your actual documents, not the model's training data.

Multi-Agent Systems
Orchestrating swarms of specialized agents to handle complex, asynchronous tasks across your organization.
→ In practice: a multi-step process — say, intake, validation, and routing — runs end to end without a person babysitting each handoff.

Predictive Intelligence
Time-series forecasting and anomaly detection running at the edge for real-time operational insights.
→ In practice: you get a flag before a metric breaks its normal range, not a report explaining it after the fact.

Air-Gapped Deployment
Zero-trust environments. Models deployed securely on-premise or within isolated cloud enclaves.
→ In practice: your data never crosses into a third-party API — the model runs where your data already lives.

API-First Architecture
Headless intelligence. Integrate our capabilities directly into your existing software stack with comprehensive REST/gRPC endpoints.
→ In practice: this plugs into the tools your team already uses instead of asking them to learn a new interface.

Each "in practice" line is one sentence — keep it that short so the cards don't get heavy.

7. Performance Telemetry section

This is the section to be most careful with. Presented as historical results ("$87.5M capital saved... in 2024," "47.2% average efficiency gain," "0 data breaches"), these numbers currently read as a track record you don't have evidence for on the page — and it's the section a skeptical enterprise buyer will scrutinize hardest. You have two honest options:

Option A — you have at least one real number or pilot to point to. Replace the section with that one real, attributable result, named or anonymized ("a mid-market logistics client reduced manual routing time by X% in a Q2 2026 pilot"). One true, specific number beats four impressive-sounding unattributed ones.

Option B — there's no client history yet. Replace the "track record" framing with a capability/target framing instead — still confident, but honest about what it is:

What to expect
Built for measurable impact

<15ms
TARGET INFERENCE LATENCY
For edge-deployed quantized models, based on internal benchmarks

Zero third-party exposure
By design
Air-gapped and VPC deployment options mean your data never leaves your infrastructure

[Your pilot engagement, here]
We're currently taking on a small number of pilot engagements — book an architecture review to be considered.

The key change is the labeling: "target" and "by design" are honest claims about the system's architecture; "$87.5M capital saved... in 2024" is a claim about business outcomes you'd need a client to vouch for.
